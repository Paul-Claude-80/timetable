Price question? Tap here
At the start (most calls)"I can definitely help you with that. Just a few quick questions." Then carry on with the next step.
Won't give their last nameDon't get stuck. Roll on: "Okay, is this for yourself or a child?" Get the last name later, at the booking.
Pushy, halfway throughDon't stop or argue. Roll to the next question: "Okay, so is this for yourself or a child?"
"I want the prices first""I completely understand. We get enquiries all day and we do book out fast, and it is hard to explain over the phone. The best way is to get you booked in, so you can come down, try it and see the place. It's easier to explain here at reception, where we've got the price sheet. I've got a spot on ___ at ___. Did you want me to pencil you in?"
Still not booking"Look, we do book out fast, so why don't we just pencil you in? At least that secures the spot. If you want to change it, we can change it later. How does that sound?"
PT or one-on-one"It depends on the style and the coach. PTs start from $99 an hour. But you might not need one, because the introduction lesson is semi-private, and it's free."
Never sayAny other dollar figure, contract or term length, joining fees, "it's our policy", "we don't give prices over the phone", "I'm not allowed to tell you".
Tricky moment? Tap here
"Can I come in today?" (it's full)"Today's full. We only take a few new people a class so the coach can look after you. I've got ___ or ___. Which is better for you?"
Not ready to bookGet their name and mobile, then agree a callback: "How about I give you a call in a couple of weeks, when things are less busy for you?" After the call, add a "New Prospect" contact log in Mindbody with "Follow up by this date" set to that day.
"I'll have to check with my partner""No problem. Let's pencil in a spot so you don't miss out. If it doesn't suit, just let us know."
Boxing, kickboxing, karate, Taekwondo, judoBoxing or kickboxing: "Muay Thai is perfect. It's boxing with the kicks, knees and elbows. Have you trained in martial arts before?" Boxed for years? "Sounds like your hands are pretty good. Now it's time to work on your kicks. Let's go ahead and get you booked in. I've got two spots available, ___ or ___. Which one is better for you?" Karate or Taekwondo: Muay Thai. Judo: jiu-jitsu. Anything else: ask their goals and suggest the best fit. Never start with "no".
"Do you do casual sessions?"First: "Do you live in Melbourne?" Visiting (here less than 3 months): "For people visiting Melbourne, we've got a casual pass." Then carry on with the script. Local: "Then let's get you booked in for an introduction lesson. It's like a free trial anyway." Never a flat no.
Rude callerStay calm: "I'm sorry it's been frustrating. Let me see what I can do." Never argue or mention the recording.
